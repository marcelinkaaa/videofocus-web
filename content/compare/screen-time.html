Short answer No. Screen Time treats YouTube as one app or one website. It can limit it, block it, or allow it, but it can't see or choose channels. Use Screen Time to lock down the device, and an allowlist app to decide what plays.

What Screen Time can do with YouTube

  • Limit it: an App Limit on the YouTube app, such as 30 minutes a day.
  • Block it: turn the app off under Allowed Apps & Features, and block youtube.com under App Store, Media, Web & Games › Web Content.
  • Schedule it: Downtime makes YouTube unavailable during set hours.
  • Stop new installs: so a blocked app can't simply be downloaded again.

What it can't do

  • Allow some channels and not others.
  • Turn off YouTube's recommendations, autoplay or Shorts feed.
  • Set limits that differ by what's being watched.

The setup

  1. In Screen Time on the child's device: turn off the YouTube app and Safari under Allowed Apps & Features.
  2. Under App Store, Media, Web & Games › Web Content, choose Only Approved Websites and add www.youtube.com. VideoFocus uses YouTube's official player, which loads from there. What this opens up, and how to keep it tight.
  3. Set app installs to Don't Allow.
  4. In VideoFocus, approve the channels you want and set a daily limit and bedtime.

Questions parents ask

Can Screen Time block YouTube Shorts?

No. Screen Time can't tell Shorts from other YouTube videos. Removing the YouTube app and using an app with no Shorts feed is the practical route.

If I allow youtube.com, can my child reach all of YouTube?

Through a browser, yes, so turn browsers off on a child's device. Inside VideoFocus, only approved channels and videos play. Full explanation.
