Short answer YouTube Kids is a separate app for young children. Supervised YouTube (set up through Google Family Link) opens regular YouTube at a content level you choose: Explore, Explore More or Most of YouTube. Both are filters. If you want your child to see only specific channels, including ones that aren't in YouTube Kids, you need an allowlist app such as VideoFocus.

What supervised YouTube does well

  • It's free and built into YouTube, on every device your child signs in to.
  • It grows with your child: you can move up a level as they get older.
  • For teens, recent updates added a daily Shorts limit, bedtime reminders and more visibility for parents.

Where it falls short

  • It's a blocklist. Everything at the chosen level is open until you find it and block it.
  • The recommendation feed is still there. The algorithm still picks what comes next, and that's where rabbit holes start.
  • The jump between levels is big. Many parents of 9 to 12 year olds find YouTube Kids too young and Explore too open.

Where an allowlist fits

An allowlist gives a 9 year old the "real YouTube" channels they care about (science, drawing, building, sport) without the rest of the site. Approve a channel once and its new uploads appear. There's no feed, no autoplay and no comments, and you can widen the list as they grow.

Questions parents ask

What's the difference between YouTube Kids and supervised YouTube?

YouTube Kids is a separate app with its own catalog for young children. Supervised YouTube is regular YouTube with content limited to a level you choose, managed through Google Family Link.

Can I limit a supervised account to specific channels?

No. Supervised accounts let you block channels, not restrict a child to a list. To allow only certain channels you need YouTube Kids' approved mode (catalog permitting) or an allowlist app.

Does my child need a Google account for VideoFocus?

No. You sign in with your email. Your child never needs a Google or YouTube account.
