Where YouTube Kids is the better choice

  • You want it on the TV. YouTube Kids runs on smart TVs and streaming boxes; VideoFocus is iPhone and iPad only.
  • Your kids are small and their favorites are in its catalog. For a 3 year old who watches Sesame Street and Super Simple Songs, approved-content mode does the job for free.
  • You're on Android. VideoFocus isn't. Yet.

Where VideoFocus is the better choice

  • Your child has outgrown "baby YouTube" but you're not ready for the whole site. You can approve "real YouTube" channels such as Mark Rober or Crash Course Kids one at a time.
  • You don't want an algorithm deciding at all. Filters miss things, including the wave of AI-generated "slop" videos reported in YouTube Kids in 2026. An allowlist can't surface what you never added.
  • You want limits that hold. Daily limits per weekday, a bedtime, and extra time only with your passcode, in the same app.
  • Approving one video at a time is wearing you out. Approve a channel and its new uploads keep coming.

Questions parents ask

Is VideoFocus a replacement for YouTube Kids?

For many families, yes, especially once kids are about 6 or older and want channels YouTube Kids doesn't carry. Some families use both: YouTube Kids on the TV and VideoFocus on the iPad.

Does VideoFocus use YouTube's player?

Yes. Every video plays in YouTube's official embedded player. VideoFocus never downloads or re-hosts videos, so creators still get their views.

Is VideoFocus made by YouTube?

No. VideoFocus is an independent app from Venture Ink LLC and isn't affiliated with or endorsed by YouTube or Google.
