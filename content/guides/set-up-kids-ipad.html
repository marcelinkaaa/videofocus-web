The 20-minute version Create a child Apple Account in Family Sharing, turn on Ask to Buy, set up Screen Time from your own iPhone (downtime, app limits, content restrictions and a Screen Time passcode only you know), turn off Safari and the YouTube app, and give your child a video app that only plays what you've approved.

A new iPad is a lot easier to set up before it's handed over than after. Do this the night before, with the iPad charged and next to your iPhone. Menu names below are from current iOS and iPadOS; they move occasionally between versions.

1. Give your child their own Apple Account

  1. On your iPhone: Settings › [your name] › Family › Add Member › Create Child Account.
  2. Sign in to the iPad with that child account, not yours. That's what lets you manage it from your phone.
  3. Recent versions of iOS can set up a child's device for you if you start the iPad next to your iPhone. Accept that; it walks through most of the steps below.

2. Turn on Ask to Buy

In Settings › Family › [child's name] › Ask to Buy. Every app download, free or paid, sends a request to your phone first.

3. Set up Screen Time from your phone

Go to Settings › Screen Time on your iPhone, tap your child's name under Family, and set:

  • Downtime: the hours the iPad is off-limits (bedtime and school mornings work well).
  • App Limits: daily time for categories such as Games and Social.
  • A Screen Time passcode that's different from the iPad unlock code. Kids learn unlock codes fast.

4. Content & Privacy Restrictions

In the same Screen Time page, open Content & Privacy Restrictions and turn it on.

  • iTunes & App Store Purchases: set Installing Apps and Deleting Apps to Don't Allow once the apps you want are installed.
  • Allowed Apps: turn off Safari if your child doesn't need a browser. Most young kids don't.
  • Content Restrictions › Apps: choose an age rating that fits.
  • Web Content: Only Approved Websites is the tightest setting. If you use VideoFocus, add www.youtube.com so the official player can load; here's what that does and doesn't open up.

5. Decide what to do about YouTube

This is the step most checklists skip, and it's where most of the screen time goes. You have three real choices:

  • YouTube Kids: free, but its catalog and filters decide what's suitable, and older kids outgrow it quickly.
  • No YouTube at all: simplest, though you lose the good stuff: Sesame Street, SciShow Kids, Art for Kids Hub.
  • Only the channels you choose: install VideoFocus, add a handful of channels (our ages 3–5 and ages 6–8 lists are a quick start), set a daily limit and bedtime, and keep the YouTube app off the iPad. Full walkthrough here.

6. Before you wrap it

  • Update iPadOS, so it doesn't ask on the morning.
  • Charge it fully and put a sturdy case on.
  • Download a few things that work offline for car rides.
  • Open each app once so the first-run screens are out of the way.

Questions parents ask

Should a young child have their own Apple Account?

Yes. A child account under Family Sharing is what makes Ask to Buy and remote Screen Time management work. Signing the iPad in with your own account gives your child your purchases, messages and photos.

Can I manage the iPad from my iPhone?

Yes. Screen Time and Ask to Buy are managed from your phone once the iPad uses a child account in your Family Sharing group. In VideoFocus, you can also add channels from your phone and they appear on the iPad.

How much screen time is right?

There's no single number. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends a family media plan built around sleep, play and school rather than one fixed limit. Daily limits that differ on weekdays and weekends tend to stick better than one rule for every day.
