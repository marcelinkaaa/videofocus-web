In one sentence

VideoFocus is an iPhone and iPad app that lets parents choose exactly which YouTube channels and videos their kids can watch, with no feed, no autoplay and screen time limits that hold.

The story

Marcelina built VideoFocus after realizing every kids' video app worked the same way: an algorithm decides what's appropriate and parents block what slips through. [One or two sentences in your own words: the moment that started it, such as a specific video your child landed on.]

VideoFocus flips that. It starts empty. Parents add the channels and videos they trust (any YouTube channel, not just a kids catalog) and kids explore freely inside that list. Approving a channel keeps its new uploads coming, and daily limits, bedtime and a parent passcode keep the rules in the app instead of in an argument.

Videos play in YouTube's official player, so creators keep their views. VideoFocus sells its own parental controls, never access to YouTube, and has no ads or third-party trackers of its own.

What makes it different

  • Choosing, not filtering: nothing plays unless a parent added it.
  • Any channel: parents can approve general-audience channels that YouTube Kids doesn't carry, which matters for kids aged 7 to 13.
  • Whole channels stay current: approve once, and new uploads appear.
  • Screen time built in: a different limit each weekday, bedtime, and extra time only with the parent passcode.
  • One price for the family, with a real free tier.
  • A track record: it applies the approach of PodFocus, the founder's parent-approved podcast app for kids, to video.
