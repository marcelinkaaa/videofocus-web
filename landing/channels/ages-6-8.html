The best YouTube channels for 6 to 8 year olds

Updated 16 November 2026 · by the parent who builds VideoFocus

Six to eight is when kids start outgrowing YouTube Kids and asking for "real YouTube". These channels are made for curious early readers and give them something worth watching, without the feed.

How we picked: real learning value, creators with a long track record, and pacing that doesn't rely on shock or clickbait. Watch a couple of videos before approving. Several of these aren't aimed only at kids, so they may not be in the YouTube Kids catalog, but you can approve them in VideoFocus.

  • SciShow Kidsages 5–9

    Short, friendly science explainers with simple experiments to try at home.

  • Crash Course Kidsages 7–10

    Earth science, engineering and the scientific method, matched to what's taught in early grades.

  • National Geographic Kidsages 6–10

    Animals, places and the natural world, from a name parents already trust.

  • Peekaboo Kidzages 5–9

    Home of "Dr. Binocs", animated answers to big questions: volcanoes, the solar system, how the body works.

  • Mystery Scienceages 6–10

    Question-led science lessons ("Why do we have seasons?") made for classrooms and great at home.

  • Art for Kids Hubages 4–10

    Step-by-step drawing with a dad and his kids. One of the best uses of a screen: it ends with a drawing.

  • Free Schoolages 6–10

    Short introductions to famous artists, composers and classical music, and to science and nature topics.

  • Homeschool Popages 6–10

    Clear, upbeat lessons on history, geography, science and math. Popular with homeschool families.

  • Storyline Onlineages 4–9

    Well-known picture books read aloud by actors, with the illustrations on screen. From the SAG-AFTRA Foundation.

  • Mark Roberages 8+

    Engineering projects and big experiments from a former NASA engineer. Long videos, best for the older end of this range.

Questions parents ask

Is YouTube Kids still right for a 7 year old?

For some kids, yes. Many find it too young by 7 or 8, and several channels on this list aren't in its catalog. An allowlist app lets you approve "real YouTube" channels one at a time instead of opening the whole site.

What about Minecraft and gaming channels?

Gaming channels vary a lot, even within one creator. Approve individual channels you've watched, and revisit the list every few months. In VideoFocus, removing a channel takes it out of the library immediately.
