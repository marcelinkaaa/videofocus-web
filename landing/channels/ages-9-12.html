The best YouTube channels for 9 to 12 year olds

Updated 23 November 2026 · by the parent who builds VideoFocus

By nine, most kids have outgrown YouTube Kids and want "real YouTube". The good news: some of the best channels on the platform are perfect for this age. These are ones we'd happily approve, so tweens get the good stuff without the recommendation feed.

How we picked: real learning value, creators with a long track record, and videos that make kids want to try something themselves. Most of these aren't made only for kids, so they may not be in the YouTube Kids catalog, but you can approve them in VideoFocus. Watch a couple of videos first.

  • Mark Roberages 8+@MarkRober

    Big engineering projects and experiments from a former NASA engineer. Squirrel obstacle courses, glitter bombs, world records.

  • CrunchLabsages 8+@CrunchLabs

    Mark Rober's build-along engineering for kids: how toys and gadgets really work.

  • America's Test Kitchen Kidsages 8+@AmericasTestKitchenKids

    Kid-tested recipes and real cooking skills from the America's Test Kitchen team. Ends in the kitchen, not on the couch.

  • TED-Edages 10+@TEDEd

    Short animated lessons on history, science, language, and its famously tricky riddles.

  • SciShowages 10+@SciShow

    Quick, well-researched science explainers from the team behind SciShow Kids. A natural next step.

  • The Action Labages 9+@TheActionLab

    Hands-on physics and chemistry experiments that answer "why does that happen?"

  • Deep Lookages 8+@KQEDDeepLook

    Short, beautifully filmed close-ups of tiny creatures and how they live, from PBS station KQED.

  • Jeremy Shaferages 8+@jeremyshaferorigami

    Origami tutorials to fold along with, from simple to seriously impressive. The screen leads to paper.

  • Brave Wildernessages 10+@BraveWilderness

    Up-close animal encounters in the wild. Some episodes feature stings and bites, so it suits the older end of this range.

Questions parents ask

Is YouTube Kids too young for a 10 year old?

For many kids, yes, and several of the best channels for this age aren't in its catalog. An allowlist app lets you approve "real YouTube" channels one at a time instead of opening the whole site.

What about gaming and Minecraft channels?

They vary a lot, even within one creator. Approve individual channels you've watched, and revisit the list every few months. In VideoFocus, removing a channel takes it out of the library immediately.
