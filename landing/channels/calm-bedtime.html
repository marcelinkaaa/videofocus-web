Calm YouTube channels for kids: bedtime and wind-down picks

Updated 16 November 2026 · by the parent who builds VideoFocus

The last screen of the day sets the tone for bedtime. Bright, fast, cliff-hanger videos make "one more" impossible. These channels are slow, quiet and kind, and they end.

How we picked: slow pacing, soft audio, no cliff-hangers. Even calm channels post the occasional upbeat video, so preview a few first.

  • Storyline Onlineages 4–9

    Picture books read aloud by actors, with the illustrations on screen. The closest thing to a bedtime story on YouTube.

  • Brightly Storytimeages 3–7

    Gentle read-alouds of picture books, at a pace that's easy to follow.

  • Moshi Kidsages 3–8

    Sleep stories, soundscapes and mindfulness from the Moshi sleep app.

  • Cosmic Kids Yogaages 3–8

    Look for the "Peace Out" relaxation videos: short guided breathing and visualization for kids.

  • Super Simple Songsages 1–5

    Their lullaby and quiet-song collections are soft and unhurried.

  • Sesame Streetages 2–5

    Has a run of calm-down and mindfulness clips with familiar characters.

  • GoNoodleages 4–10

    Best known for dance breaks, but its calming and breathing videos work well for winding down.

  • Free Schoolages 6–10

    Gentle introductions to classical music and composers, good as quiet background before lights out.

Make the routine stick

  • Make a separate wind-down playlist. In VideoFocus, put a few of these in a playlist and make it the evening option.
  • Let the app say no. Set a bedtime in VideoFocus and playback locks at that time, so you're not the one ending the video.
  • Stop 30–60 minutes before lights out where you can. Pediatric sleep advice consistently recommends screens off before bed.
  • Switch to audio for the last stretch. Our other app, PodFocus, does the same thing for podcasts: only shows you approved, with a daily limit. Sleep stories and calm shows with the screen face-down.

Questions parents ask

Are bedtime videos bad for sleep?

Most sleep guidance recommends turning screens off before bed. If a video is part of your routine, slow, quiet content that ends well before lights out is the gentler option, and a fixed cut-off time helps.
