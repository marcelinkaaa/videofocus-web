The best YouTube Kids alternatives for iPhone and iPad (an honest list)

Updated 16 November 2026 · by the parent who builds VideoFocus

How to choose Decide first whether you want a curated catalog (someone else picks safe videos, like PBS KIDS Video) or an allowlist (you pick the YouTube channels, like VideoFocus or Safe Vision). Then check three things: whether new uploads from approved channels appear automatically, whether screen time is built in, and what it costs for all your kids.

We make VideoFocus, which is on this list. Prices and ratings are from the US App Store on 16 November 2026 and change often. The drawbacks listed for other apps come from patterns in their public App Store reviews.

Google's own options

YouTube Kids, approved-content mode

Free, on every screen including TVs, and parents approve videos, channels or collections. The catch: only what's in the YouTube Kids catalog, and search is off. Common problems.

Supervised YouTube (Family Link)

Regular YouTube at a content level you choose. Free and grows with your child, but it's a blocklist with the recommendation feed still on. How it compares.

Curated catalogs (someone else picks)

  • PBS KIDS Video: free clips and full episodes from PBS shows. Excellent for preschoolers; no YouTube channels.
  • GoNoodle: free movement and mindfulness videos, popular in classrooms.

Our honest take

  • Toddlers, and you want free: PBS KIDS Video, or YouTube Kids in approved mode.
  • You need it on the TV: YouTube Kids.
  • Ages 5–13 on iPad or iPhone, and you want to pick the channels yourself, with limits that hold: an allowlist app. We built VideoFocus for exactly this, and it's free to try for one child.

Questions parents ask

What is the safest alternative to YouTube Kids?

The safest setup is an allowlist, where nothing plays unless a parent approved it, combined with Screen Time blocking the YouTube app and browsers. Filters, including YouTube Kids' age levels, can miss things.

Is there a free YouTube Kids alternative?

Yes. PBS KIDS Video and GoNoodle are free curated catalogs. Several allowlist apps, VideoFocus included, have a free tier for one child.

Which alternatives work for older kids and tweens?

Allowlist apps work best for 9 to 13 year olds, because you can approve general-audience channels that YouTube Kids doesn't carry, without opening up all of YouTube.
