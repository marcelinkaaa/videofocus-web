New iPad for Christmas? Set it up before you wrap it

Updated 25 November 2026 · by the parent who builds VideoFocus

The short version Open the box a few days before Christmas, sign the iPad in with your child's own Apple Account, set up Screen Time from your phone, decide what to do about YouTube, then put it back in the box. On Christmas morning your child gets a device that's ready to use, and you don't spend the day in Settings.

Most of the setup takes about 20 minutes, but it's the kind of 20 minutes nobody has on Christmas morning, with a child waiting and an update downloading. Doing it early also means the first time your child opens the iPad, the rules are already there. Removing things later is a much harder conversation than never adding them.

This page is the holiday plan. For every setting step by step, use our full kid's iPad checklist.

A week or so before: set it up

Open the box and charge it. Apple's paper seal tears when you open it, so the box won't look factory-sealed, but everything goes back in neatly. With your iPhone next to it:

  1. Create a child Apple Account in Settings › Family on your iPhone, and sign the iPad in with that account rather than yours.
  2. Turn on Ask to Buy, so every download asks you first.
  3. Set up Screen Time from your phone: downtime for bedtime, app limits, content restrictions and a Screen Time passcode that's different from the iPad's unlock code.
  4. Update iPadOS now, so it doesn't ask on the morning.
  5. Install the apps you've chosen and open each one once to get the welcome screens out of the way.

The full checklist has the exact menus for each of these.

Decide what to do about YouTube

This is the decision that shapes the iPad most, and the defaults work against you: the YouTube app is built to recommend the next video, and the next. You have three real options:

  • YouTube Kids: free, but its catalog and filters decide what's suitable, and it's designed for younger children.
  • No YouTube at all: the simplest choice, though you lose a lot of good channels.
  • Only the channels you choose: install VideoFocus and keep the YouTube app off the iPad. Your child gets a library of channels you approved, with no feed and no autoplay into the unknown, plus a daily limit and a bedtime.

Put it back in the box

  • Charge it to 100% and put a sturdy case on. A case often fits in the box, or can be its own small present.
  • Leave it signed in and locked, then re-box and wrap.
  • If it's going to grandparents or another home first, write the unlock code inside the card, not on the box.

Christmas morning

  • Hand it over with the rules, not after. "It lives in the kitchen at night" is easier to say on day one than on day ten.
  • Show them what's in their library. Let them pick a favorite channel to watch first, so the iPad starts as something they chose with you.
  • Plan for the long, slow days. The week after Christmas is when screen time tends to creep. Decide now what a normal holiday day looks like. Audio is a good screen-free option for car rides and quiet time: PodFocus, from the same team, plays only the podcasts you approve.

The first week

Look at what they actually watched (in VideoFocus, the family dashboard shows it), and adjust. Add a channel they asked for, remove one that didn't land, move the bedtime if the holiday schedule changes. The American Academy of Pediatrics suggests making a family media plan and says every family and child is different. (Source: HealthyChildren.org, American Academy of Pediatrics) Its free Family Media Plan tool is a good way to write your rules down together.

Questions parents ask

Can I set up an iPad and still wrap it as new?

Yes. The box's paper seal will be broken, but the iPad, cable and case go back in neatly, and your child still gets to unwrap it. The difference is that it's ready the moment they turn it on.

Should a young child have their own Apple Account?

Yes. A child account under Family Sharing is what makes Ask to Buy and managing Screen Time from your phone work. Signing the iPad in with your own account gives your child access to your purchases, messages and photos.

Can my child just download the YouTube app?

Not if you stop it. With Ask to Buy on, every download comes to your phone for approval. In Screen Time's Content & Privacy Restrictions you can also set Installing Apps to Don't Allow, which removes the App Store from the iPad. Our full checklist explains both.
